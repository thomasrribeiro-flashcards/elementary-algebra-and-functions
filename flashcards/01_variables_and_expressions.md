# Variables and expressions

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000001 -->
Q: A **variable** is a letter or another symbol that represents a number. In \(n+3\), let \(n\) represent the number of counters in a closed bag. If the bag contains \(5\) counters, which number should replace \(n\)?
A: \(5\). The variable \(n\) stands for the bag's counter count.

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000002 -->
Q: What does a variable represent?
A: A number. Its value may be given, unknown, or allowed to change.

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000003 -->
Q: An **algebraic expression** combines numbers, variables, and operation symbols to represent a quantity; it does not make an equality claim. Which item is an algebraic expression: \(n+3\) or \(n+3=8\)?
A: \(n+3\). The item \(n+3=8\) makes an equality claim.

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000004 -->
Q: An **equation** is a statement that two expressions are equal, shown with \(=\). Which item is an equation: \(n+3\) or \(n+3=8\)?
A: \(n+3=8\). It states that the expressions \(n+3\) and \(8\) have equal values.

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000005 -->
Q: When a number and variable are written together, the multiplication sign is often omitted: \(4x\) means \(4\times x\). If \(x=3\), which arithmetic multiplication does \(4x\) represent?
A: \(4\times3\). The value \(3\) replaces \(x\).

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000006 -->
Q: A **term** is a part of an expression separated by addition or subtraction. What are the two terms in \(4x+7\)?
A: \(4x\) and \(7\).

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000007 -->
Q: A **coefficient** is the numerical factor multiplying a variable. What is the coefficient of \(x\) in \(4x\)?
A: \(4\). The term means \(4\times x\).

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000008 -->
Q: A **constant term** has no variable, so its value stays fixed when the variable changes. What is the constant term in \(4x+7\)?
A: \(7\).

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000009 -->
Q: To **substitute** \(x=3\) into \(4x+7\), replace \(x\) with \(3\), producing \(4\times3+7\). What value does the expression have?
A: \(19\), because \(4\times3+7=12+7=19\).

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000010 -->
Q: To **evaluate** an algebraic expression is to find its value. When a value is given for a variable, what must replace every occurrence of that variable before the operations are evaluated?
A: The given value. Then follow the established order of operations.

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000011 -->
P: Evaluate \(5a-2\) when \(a=4\). The substitution step gives \(5\times4-2\).
S: **IDENTIFY:** Evaluate an expression at a given variable value.

**PLAN:** Substitute \(a=4\), then follow the order of operations.

**EXECUTE:** \(5\times4-2=20-2=18\).

**EVALUATE:** Since \(5\times3=15\) and \(5\times5=25\), a nearby result of \(18\) is reasonable.

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000012 -->
Q: Within one evaluation, every occurrence of the same variable has the same chosen value. If \(x=2\), why does \(x+x\) become \(2+2\), not \(2+3\)?
A: Both occurrences are the same variable \(x\), so both must be replaced by its chosen value \(2\).

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000013 -->
Q: The phrase “\(7\) more than \(n\)” means start with \(n\) and add \(7\). Which expression represents it?
A: \(n+7\).

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000014 -->
Q: The phrase “\(5\) less than \(n\)” means subtract \(5\) from \(n\). Which expression represents it?
A: \(n-5\). The order matters: \(5-n\) means \(n\) less than \(5\).

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000015 -->
Q: The phrase “\(6\) times \(m\)” means \(6\times m\). How is this product usually written without a multiplication sign?
A: \(6m\).

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000016 -->
P: Four identical packets each contain \(c\) cards, and there are \(3\) additional loose cards. Write an expression for the total, then evaluate it when \(c=5\).
S: **IDENTIFY:** Four equal groups give multiplication; the loose cards are then added.

**PLAN:** Write \(4c+3\), then substitute \(c=5\).

**EXECUTE:** \(4(5)+3=20+3=23\) cards.

**EVALUATE:** Four packets of \(5\) account for \(20\) cards, and the \(3\) loose cards give \(23\), matching the context.

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000017 -->
Q: A learner claims that \(4x\) and \(x+4\) always have the same value because both contain \(x\) and \(4\). Diagnose the mistake using \(x=2\).
A: \(4x\) means multiplication, while \(x+4\) means addition. At \(x=2\), their values are \(8\) and \(6\), so they are not always equal.

<!-- card-id: 01000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000018 -->
P: The table records chosen values of \(x\) and the corresponding value of \(2x+1\). Complete the blank.

| \(x\) | value of \(2x+1\) |
|---:|---:|
| \(1\) | \(3\) |
| \(4\) | ? |
S: Substitute \(x=4\): \(2(4)+1=8+1=9\). The missing value is \(9\). Check with inverse operations: \((9-1)\div2=4\), recovering the row's chosen value of \(x\).
