# Variables and expressions

## F01 — A variable with a concrete value

<!-- card-id: card-abd5146b-e778-4ed9-b954-f96919067dcd -->
Q: A letter can stand for a number. Here, \(x\) stands for \(4\), and the letter \(x\) is called a **variable**. What number does \(x\) represent?
A: **4**. In this statement, the variable \(x\) stands for the number \(4\).

## F02 — Meaning of variable

<!-- card-id: card-0eb26239-aee0-441d-9021-2c8cdf87edea -->
Q: What is a **variable**?
A: **A letter that stands for a number.** The number it stands for is the variable's value.

## F03 — From arithmetic to an algebraic expression

<!-- card-id: card-0aa1d534-7044-47fa-a54f-c7768dd77cb6 -->
Q: An arithmetic expression combines numbers and operation signs. An **algebraic expression** may also contain a variable. Which kind of expression is \(3+x\)?
A: **An algebraic expression.** It contains the variable \(x\) as well as a number and an addition sign.

## F04 — Reading an algebraic expression

<!-- card-id: card-2281a3e3-4aaa-4255-bc53-9b60a452f546 -->
Q: What calculation does the algebraic expression \(3+x\) describe?
A: **Add \(3\) to the number represented by \(x\).** An expression describes a quantity to calculate; it does not by itself ask for a particular value of \(x\).

## F05 — First equation

<!-- card-id: card-bc8df31f-3ebc-45ce-a6cf-a9b4e723a1f6 -->
Q: An **equation** is a mathematical statement that uses an equals sign to say two quantities have the same value. Which item is an equation: \(3+x\) or \(3+x=8\)?
A: **\(3+x=8\) is the equation.** It uses an equals sign to state that the two quantities have the same value.

## F06 — Expression versus equation

<!-- card-id: card-0e76a07e-ad97-495d-a002-d84e9cb48cc6 -->
Q: What is the decisive difference between an algebraic expression and an equation?
A: **An expression names a quantity; an equation states that two quantities have the same value.** An equation includes an equals sign, while an expression alone does not.

## F07 — Terms

<!-- card-id: card-264513ff-fa38-440e-9890-86b81dcebc52 -->
Q: An algebraic expression can be read as terms joined by addition or subtraction. In the simple expression \(x+7\), the addition sign separates two **terms**. What are they?
A: **\(x\) and \(7\).** The addition sign separates those two terms.

## F08 — Coefficient and multiplication notation

<!-- card-id: card-47b9bf7b-9eeb-4194-83b5-5de0aacb4bfd -->
Q: When a number is written next to a variable, \(4x\) means \(4\times x\). The number multiplying a variable is its **coefficient**. What is the coefficient in \(4x\)?
A: **\(4\).** It is the number multiplying the variable \(x\).

## F09 — Constant term

<!-- card-id: card-0ca21952-e347-44f7-8a81-74217a14a9dd -->
Q: In \(4x+7\), the terms are \(4x\) and \(7\). A term with no variable is called a **constant term**. Which term is the constant term?
A: **\(7\).** It has no variable, so its value stays fixed within the expression.

## F10 — Coefficient versus term

<!-- card-id: card-54d4a2aa-74f2-4da5-a1e3-902d6a79a194 -->
Q: A learner says, “The coefficient in \(6n+2\) is \(6n\).” What is the correction?
A: **The coefficient is \(6\); \(6n\) is the whole term.** The coefficient is only the number multiplying the variable.

## F11 — Substitution

<!-- card-id: card-f575b8be-723e-48cc-9e65-1d826c78e386 -->
Q: **Substitution** means replacing a variable with its given number. If \(x=3\), what arithmetic expression results when you substitute into \(x+5\)?
A: **\(3+5\).** Replace \(x\) with \(3\) and keep the stated addition.

## F12 — Evaluating an expression

<!-- card-id: card-3bd06661-ff4b-4097-8e1f-9eac27201551 -->
Q: To **evaluate** an expression means to calculate its value. What is the value of \(x+5\) when \(x=3\)?
A: **\(8\).** Substitute \(3\) for \(x\), then calculate \(3+5=8\).

## F13 — Analyzed substitution problem

<!-- card-id: card-1149b5bf-f10e-4710-944c-659ab7cb9737 -->
P: Evaluate \(2x+1\) when \(x=3\).
S:
**IDENTIFY**

This is expression evaluation by substitution. The given value is \(x=3\).

**PLAN**

Replace \(x\) with \(3\), interpret \(2x\) as multiplication, and follow the established operation order.

**EXECUTE**

**The value is \(7\).** Substitution gives \(2\times3+1\), so multiplication gives \(6+1=7\).

**EVALUATE**

Undo the final addition: \(7-1=6\), which matches \(2\times3\).

## F14 — Faded substitution problem

<!-- card-id: card-c5e36f89-8d04-4195-adf1-1e0bb86fa334 -->
P: Evaluate \(4m+3\) when \(m=5\).
S:
**IDENTIFY**

This is expression evaluation with the given value \(m=5\).

**PLAN**

Substitute, then use operation order.

**EXECUTE**

**The value is \(23\).** \(4\times5+3=20+3=23\).

**EVALUATE**

Subtracting the constant term gives \(23-3=20\), the same as \(4\times5\).

## F15 — Translating addition

<!-- card-id: card-d7dd4b63-2563-4777-9dcf-07fc625b50e1 -->
Q: Let \(n\) stand for a number. Which algebraic expression records “add \(6\) to the number”?
A: **\(n+6\).** The variable \(n\) represents the number, and \(+6\) records the addition.

## F16 — Translating two operations

<!-- card-id: card-8d385e3a-d53d-431c-9334-67f5177ea540 -->
Q: Let \(p\) stand for a number. Which expression records “multiply the number by \(3\), then add \(2\)”?
A: **\(3p+2\).** The term \(3p\) means \(3\times p\), and the constant term \(2\) is then added.

## F17 — Independent contextual translation and evaluation

<!-- card-id: card-52403106-2545-40d4-a786-99de42fc09b0 -->
P: Each packet contains \(b\) counters. There are three packets and two extra counters. If \(b=4\), use an algebraic expression to find the total number of counters.
S:
**IDENTIFY**

The repeated packet amount is represented by \(b\), and the two extra counters form an added constant term.

**PLAN**

Translate the situation into an expression, substitute \(b=4\), and evaluate.

**EXECUTE**

**There are \(14\) counters.** The expression is \(3b+2\), and substitution gives \(3\times4+2=14\).

**EVALUATE**

The inverse check \((14-2)\div3=4\) recovers the stated four counters per packet.

## F18 — Tabular transfer

<!-- card-id: card-4df6c3c9-45ab-4d53-be75-baafe22992a8 -->
P: The table's left column gives a value for \(n\); the right column gives the value of \(n+2\). What number belongs in the blank?

| \(n\) | \(n+2\) |
|---:|---:|
| \(4\) | \(6\) |
| \(6\) | ? |

S:
**IDENTIFY**

The row gives \(n=6\), and the expression to evaluate is \(n+2\).

**PLAN**

Substitute the row's value for \(n\), then add.

**EXECUTE**

**The missing number is \(8\).** Substitution gives \(6+2=8\).

**EVALUATE**

Subtracting \(2\) from the result returns the row's given value of \(n\): \(8-2=6\).

## F19 — Subtraction order in verbal translation

<!-- card-id: card-0b09c597-3f2a-4926-aecf-ba669090f599 -->
Q: Which expression records “subtract \(n\) from \(8\)”?
A: **\(8-n\).** The quantity named after “from” comes first; \(n-8\) would reverse the subtraction.

## F20 — Diagnosing a substitution error

<!-- card-id: card-60ac8432-8f73-4a38-9ffd-dd315436330b -->
Q: For \(x=4\), a learner substitutes into \(3x+2\) and writes \(34+2\). What mistake did the learner make?
A: **They treated \(3x\) as joined digits instead of multiplication.** Because \(3x\) means \(3\times x\), the substituted expression is \(3\times4+2\).
